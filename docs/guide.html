LibreTag: a tracker that talks over MeshCore

LibreTag is an open replacement for an AirTag for your car, jacket or backpack. Instead of strangers' phones and a cloud, it uses a LoRa radio link and bridges that anyone can run. It talks mostly to the nearest bridge or your own node, and goes through the wider MeshCore mesh only when nothing nearby answers. After each report it listens for a minute in case you have sent it a command. Only you can see the content, and nothing in the packet reveals which tracker sent it.

1. Flash the firmware to the tracker

The firmware replaces whatever the tracker runs now (Meshtastic or MeshCore). The way back is the same: flash the original UF2.

  1. Connect the tracker to the computer with a USB-C cable.
  2. Press the reset button twice in quick succession. The tracker enters the bootloader and a new USB drive appears on the computer.
  3. Copy the file libretag-tracker-wio_tracker_l1.uf2 to that drive. The drive disconnects by itself and the tracker restarts into LibreTag.
  4. The display stays dark. That is correct, the tracker is saving battery. The app configures it over USB.
The drive did not appear? Try another cable (some carry power only) or press reset faster. On the first flash the tracker formats its own storage and generates a key. The version command prints the firmware version. You flash a new version the same way, a UF2 through the bootloader.

2. The LibreTag app and pairing over USB

  1. Install the LibreTag APK.
  2. In MeshCore Open turn on Local API and connect to your companion node.
  3. Open LibreTag. MeshCore Open asks whether to allow the app (you will find the fingerprint in the LibreTag menu), and then whether to add the channel #libretag. Allow both.
  4. Connect the tracker to the phone with a USB cable (USB-C, or through an OTG adapter). Android offers to open LibreTag and asks for USB access. Allow it. If nothing opens, tap Add tag.
  5. The wizard has four steps: plug in, name (a name and a mark that tells your tags apart on the map), where it rests and done. In the third step add the place where the tag usually stays, such as home or the garage, and it keeps quiet there. You can skip it and add places later. Then tap Pair.
  6. The app reads the key, sets the channel #libretag, the interval, the automatic network by country and your Nostr npub, and then locks the tracker: the USB console no longer gives the key out, and the key stays only in the app. For later settings over USB the app unlocks it by itself. The last step says what the tag will do (reports when away, quiet at your place, an alert when it is not heard or the battery is low) and offers to pick the devices you carry.
  7. Disconnect the cable. The first report arrives after the first GPS fix, then at the set interval. Tapping the tracker opens the map. A welcome message from the tracker arrives on your Nostr: add it to your contacts so you get the battery alert later.
Lost phone, or a tracker from another phone. The app's data is excluded from Android backup and from transfer to a new phone, so the key does not move over by itself. Set up Backup in the menu: LibreTag makes a 12-word recovery phrase for you to write on paper, and can also keep a copy encrypted to your Nostr npub. With either one you restore your tags on a new phone or open them in the web app. Without a backup, reset the tracker and pair it again. A tracker locked by another phone can be reset the same way: Reset pairing in the app, or unpair in the console.
The radio configures itself. After every GPS fix the tracker works out the country and sets its network: in Slovakia 869.618 / 62.5 / SF7 / CR5, 2-byte hashes in the path and the region sk. Near a border (within 3 km) it sends a second copy to the neighboring network, and only when the home network was silent. Until it has the first fix, it transmits on the default settings.

3. Tag settings and places

Everything about one tag sits on one screen, Tag settings in the tag's menu. Each row shows its value and what it does. A change that has not reached the tag yet says when it goes out by radio, or that it waits for the cable.

  1. Reports. How often the tag reports when it is away (15 minutes to 6 hours), and how often it listens for commands between reports. Those listen-only wake-ups are checks: the tag wakes, listens for about 30 seconds and goes back to sleep without GPS or transmitting, so a command reaches it within one check interval (about an hour by default) while the battery lasts longer.
  2. Quiet. The tag stays quiet in its places, while your devices are near (a watch, a ring, a beacon in the garage; pick them under Known devices with the tag on the cable) and when it has not moved for a while. While quiet it still sends a sign of life now and then. For a person, "while my devices are near" is off, because someone could take your phone along.
  3. Places. One list holds all places of a tag. A GPS place is a circle on the map: tap where the tag rests and drag the handle to change its size. A tag without GPS learns a Wi-Fi place from the networks it hears: put it there, connect the cable and snap the place, which takes about half a minute. Each place has two switches, Tell me when it leaves and Tell me when it arrives. These are ordinary notifications, not alarms.
  4. Guard. On the tag's detail, Guard this spot raises an alarm if the tag moves more than 300 m. Guard is the only alarm. The other alerts are not heard when expected and low battery, and you set both per tag.
  5. Position from Wi-Fi. A report from a Wi-Fi tag without GPS lists the networks it heard. In the app's Settings you pick a service (BeaconDB, the GrapheneOS proxy for Apple's data, or Apple directly) and when the app asks it: only when I tap, in Lost mode or every report without GPS. Each lookup tells the service which networks are near the tag. Answers are cached, and reports from your own places are never looked up.

Without a cable. With firmware 0.8.0 and Android 12 or newer the app also reaches a tag over Bluetooth. Press the tag's reset button once: it listens on Bluetooth for 3 minutes. Open Tag settings, Service or Known devices and tap Connect over Bluetooth, or Connect on the strip that shows while the tag listens. Everything the cable does then works, except a firmware update, a new key and a reset of the pairing. Press reset only once: a double press puts the tag in update mode. Switching it off and on works too. A tag with an almost empty battery, or one that keeps crashing, stays silent.

Without a restart. With firmware 0.9.2, Open Bluetooth remotely next to Connect over Bluetooth asks the tag to listen on Bluetooth at its next check. The app shows when that will be. Keep the screen open and tap Connect when the strip shows up. The request goes from your phone over Bluetooth when the tag can take it there, otherwise from your MeshCore node to radios in range only, and through Nostr bridges only when neither reaches the tag at its next check. With firmware 0.9.3 the tag looks for your phone's request at every check, so with the phone next to the tag the window opens at the very next one. If the request doesn't arrive then, the app hands it to the bridges. With firmware 0.9.5 the phone also sends a wake-up request that doesn't depend on the tag's reports, so it works even when the app has missed reports for hours. Keep the phone near the tag. The app shows when it expects the tag to wake up and waits for up to two report periods (at least 2 hours). When the tag answers, the app connects by itself, or a notification lets you know if you left the screen. Stop waiting cancels the request.

Sharing. Share in the tag's menu shows a QR code (or sends a Nostr DM) at one of three levels. View only shows positions from now on. View and control also sends commands, Lost included. Full access also lets the other phone change the tag's settings over the cable or Bluetooth, the same as yours. It's meant for your own second phone or a laptop. Whoever has that code can give the tag a new key or unpair it, and you can't take the code back, so the app asks before it shows it. On the other phone, tap Add tag and scan the code. No level reveals where the tag was before the moment you shared it.

While it listens the tag advertises from a private address with a code only your app recognises. Another phone can connect, but without your key the tag answers nothing, and nobody can tell which tag it is. If you want no Bluetooth at all, send blecon boot off on an unlocked console and use the cable.

4. The tracker console (optional)

You do not normally need it, the app does everything. For diagnostics you can also connect from a computer. A paired tracker is locked: it accepts only status, version, selftest, buzz and unpair.

  1. Open the console (on Linux usually /dev/ttyACM0): 
    screen /dev/cu.usbmodem* 115200
    Type help and Enter. Quit with Ctrl-A and then K.
  2. Status and version. Before pairing, status also prints LIBRETAG KEY …. Whoever has that key can see the tracker's position, so do not send it to anyone. After locking it prints only the identifier and locked. 
    status
version
  3. Where it would transmit. Shows the country and the networks the tracker would choose at the given place: 
    where 48.7000 21.2500
  4. Manual radio, if your network uses different settings (it turns the automatic mode off, and you turn it back on with network auto): 
    radio 869.618 62.5 7 5
  5. Unlocked from a laptop. Share the tag with Full access, copy the link (send it to yourself as a Nostr DM, or scan the QR code), and import it in the repository's host/ folder. The keys go to a file only you can read, under ~/.config/libretag/tags/, and are never printed. libretag console then finds the tag on USB, unlocks it and runs the commands you give it (or the lines you type). Over Bluetooth, tools/blecon.py run --tag does the same while the tag listens. 
    pbpaste | uv run libretag share import -
uv run libretag console -c status -c "history 0"
  6. Diagnostics: selftest checks the Nostr crypto, the GPS and the Wi-Fi scan without printing keys or the position, report sends a report right away, discover and meshscan 60 show repeaters nearby, gps the satellites, noise the radio noise, buzz 3 tries the buzzer.

5. Nostr bridge (optional)

The bridge publishes to Nostr what it picks up on the radio: LibreTag's encrypted reports (it decrypts nothing of them) and Roadstr reports. In the other direction it can transmit a command that the app left in the Nostr mailbox. You do not need it for the first tests, but it extends the range: it is enough for the tracker to be heard by any bridge. In this guide a bridge always means a MeshCore<->Nostr bridge.

  1. Install the Nostr bridge APK and turn on the Bridge running switch. MeshCore Open asks again for permission and for the channel #nostr.
  2. You set nothing else. The bridge publishes the reports itself (with a random signature, without the tracker's key), and a command from the app is transmitted by the one bridge that heard the tracker most recently. If no relay takes a report, the bridge keeps it and sends it later, even after a restart.
  3. If you want to write to the tracker from a Nostr client (NIP-04 DM), in the bridge add Settings → Add device: the tracker's npub (the app shows it in the menu Tracker's Nostr contact) and your npub, and turn on Send messages to devices.

How it works

One wake-up of the tracker

The tracker sleeps most of the time. It spends the most energy on listening, not on transmitting.

wake-upnext wake-up, interval ±10 %
0 swaits for GPS up to 90 stransmitslistens 60 s~151 s
GPS fix1 packet (under 1 s)listening for commandssleep

One packet per report

Every report is one encrypted packet, sent first as a zero-hop hello that no repeater forwards. Your app confirms it through a bridge or your own node. Only when nobody confirms and the tag moved or was silent too long, or on an event such as a command answer or low battery, the tag sends the report through the repeaters, starting in the narrowest region (near a border possibly a copy to the neighboring network). The interval has a random offset of ±10 %, so it cannot be recognized by its regularity.

LT2 encryption

The report is encrypted so that an identical counter reveals nothing. It has a rotating 4-byte tag, the key for reading is separate from the key for commands, and every 64 reports it advances (ratchet), so a stolen tracker does not reveal old positions. After pairing the key leaves the tracker and the USB console locks.

Commands after the report

The app holds the command and sends it after the tracker's report, while the tracker listens for a minute, or at the next check of a quiet tracker. It transmits it from its own radio and also leaves it in the Nostr mailbox, from where any bridge delivers it. When the phone is near the tag, it also advertises the command over Bluetooth. The tracker confirms it in its next report.

Bridges

Any bridge publishes a captured report to Nostr as an encrypted event with a random signature. It cannot read the content. That is how you see the tracker even outside the range of your own nodes. A bridge sends a command only when it is signed with the key that the tracker's last report points to. Only you and the people you gave control of the tag have that key, so a stranger cannot get a bridge to send anything to your tag.

Limits that protect the battery and the mesh. Most reports are a hello that no repeater forwards, and a tag confirmed by a bridge or your node does not flood at all. A tag that stands still floods at most 8 times in 24 hours as a fallback. Track and Lost report at most every 2 minutes. A tag that has sent 200 packets in the last 24 hours slows down to one report an hour until the count drops, but it still answers your commands. Below a safe battery voltage it does not transmit at all.
What is visible from outside. The channel #libretag is public. A radio observer sees that a LibreTag report was sent and roughly from where (by the first repeater in the path). They do not see which tracker it was or what is in the report.
A position without GPS is an area, not a single point. From the heard repeaters and the downloaded map the app draws 68 % and 95 % circles. With a poor signal they can be a kilometer wide.

Messages to the tracker over Nostr

The tracker does not accept DMs, it is controlled from the app. A command goes after its report or at its next check from your radio and also through the Nostr mailbox, from where any bridge delivers it. It writes to you by itself only when the battery is below 10 %.

When a tag is late

When a report doesn't come on time, the tag's row says the most likely reason instead of just "silent". Open the tag and the Why card under its status shows what the app based that on, the one action that settles it, and All checks: whether the phone is online, the relays, your own node, bridges that heard the tag, the last report, lost reports over 7 days, the battery, Bluetooth, the cable, restarts, the schedule, the firmware and the phone's clock.

One missed report is normal on a shared mesh, so the row first says "1 report missed" with the time of the next one. A tag counts as silent, with the orange band and a notification, after the second missed report. A guarded tag, one in Lost or Track, and a quiet tag past its heartbeat count as silent after the first.

The row saysWhat to do
In update mode · can't reportThe tag on the cable sits in its bootloader, which waits without a time limit. Choose Finish the update, or press the reset button once.
Silent since the updateAn update started from this phone and the tag hasn't reported since. Plug it in: if it is still in update mode, the app sees it.
Keeps restartingThe tag restarted several times (the phone heard it start up over Bluetooth, or its reports say so with firmware 0.9.4). A weak battery or a loose connector is the usual reason.
Phone is offline, or Can't reach the relaysThe phone can't receive anything, so it can't tell whether the tag reported. The app waits with the notification until the connection is back and it has asked the relays for what it missed.
Reported 14:31 · not on your relaysA bridge heard the tag, but its report isn't on your relays. Add relay appears when that bridge has heard your tag on two different days.
May be quiet near you, or at HomeA guess: the one report that says the tag went quiet may have been lost. Check if it's here asks the tag over Bluetooth at its next wake-up; keep the phone near the tag. A guess never turns the warning off.
With you · quiet on purposeThe tag answered over Bluetooth and said it sees one of your devices.
Battery probably emptyThe last report was at 5 % or below, or the battery trend says it ran out since. Charge it.
Sends every 2 h, app expects every 3 hThe tag and the app disagree on the schedule. Use the tag's schedule or Send the app's schedule.

Notice my tags over Bluetooth in Settings (on by default, Android 12 and newer) lets the phone hear a tag's Bluetooth signal in the background, so it notices a restart near you. It uses the phone's Bluetooth filter and no location.

When something fails

SymptomWhat to check
The app receives nothingThe radio parameters do not match the network, or MeshCore Open does not have the channel #libretag. The connection status is at the top of the app.
A report without a positionThe tracker had no GPS fix. Take it outside, the first fix takes longer.
A command keeps waitingThe tracker carries it out at its next report or check, because it sleeps most of the time. The app shows when the command goes out. If reports arrive and the command does not, it did not make it within the listening window. Try more frequent checks in Tag settings, or a longer listen.
MeshCore Open rejected the appIn MeshCore Open remove the pairing and allow the app again. Compare the fingerprint.
The tracker is locked and configuring from the console does not workThat is correct (ERR locked). The app configures it over USB. If the tracker belongs to another phone, use Reset pairing or unpair.
The position without GPS is inaccurate or missingDownload the Repeater map (refresh it after 30 days). It is only an area based on the heard repeaters. For a Wi-Fi tag, check that a service is chosen under Position from Wi-Fi. If the service does not know the networks, the app says so.

What is still missing

The tracker runs on real hardware (Wio Tracker L1), but it is still a prototype. The Wi-Fi tag without GPS runs only on the bench. The bridges and part of the Nostr direction are verified less than the tracker itself.

Not done

Seeed X1 support, Apple Find My and Google Find Hub, sending only on movement. Firmware update from the app is written but has not run on a tag yet.

To be measured

Power use (PPK2), network latency, whether Nostr clients display messages from the tracker, the accuracy of position without GPS in different cities, commands over Bluetooth from a real phone, and a Wi-Fi tag on a battery away from its places.
